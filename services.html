Services

Six things I do well.

You can book a single one or the whole chain. Most clients start with one and add the rest once they see it working.

01

Amazon & Product Development

From "I have an idea" to a product that sells on Amazon. I do this for my own brand every day, so you get the version that already survived contact with reality.

  • Niche & competitor research — demand, margins and how beatable the competition really is, based on Helium 10 data instead of gut feeling.
  • Product development & sourcing — finding manufacturers, comparing quotes, sample rounds and improving the product where the reviews of competitors show the weak spots.
  • Import, customs & logistics — getting the goods to Germany and into the FBA warehouse without expensive surprises.
  • Listing that ranks and converts — keyword research, title, bullets, description, A+ content and backend keywords.
  • Images, photos and 3D renderings — the main image decides whether anyone even clicks. Done in-house with Affinity Photo and KeyShot.
  • Launch strategy — pricing, PPC push, review build-up and what to do in the first weeks when nothing moves yet.
Helium 10Seller CentralA+ ContentFBA
02

Campaigns & Paid Advertising

Ads that are judged by what they bring in, not by how many impressions they collect. I run campaigns with my own money, so I know what a bad ACOS feels like.

  • Amazon PPC — Sponsored Products, Brands and Display: campaign structure, keyword harvesting, bid management and negative keywords that actually get maintained.
  • Google Ads & Google Shopping — search, Performance Max and Shopping including the product feed. Also international setups across several countries and languages.
  • Microsoft Advertising — the channel most people forget, often with noticeably cheaper clicks in B2B.
  • Meta Ads — Facebook and Instagram for products that need to be shown rather than searched for.
  • Ongoing optimisation — weekly or monthly, with a report you can read in five minutes: what was spent, what came back, what I changed and why.
  • Account rescue — taking over an account that someone else set up, cleaning it out and finding where the budget leaks.
Amazon PPCGoogle AdsMicrosoft AdsMeta Ads
03

SEO & SEA

Paid traffic works from day one but stops the moment you stop paying. Organic takes longer and then keeps running. Most businesses need both — the question is the ratio.

  • Technical SEO — site structure, loading speed, mobile, indexing, structured data. The unspectacular part that most sites fail at.
  • Keyword research & content planning — which terms are actually worth ranking for at your size, and what content is needed for them.
  • On-page optimisation — titles, meta descriptions, headings, internal linking and product texts that read like they were written for humans.
  • Amazon SEO — ranking inside Amazon works differently from Google. Listing structure, keyword coverage and the relationship between PPC and organic rank.
  • Local SEO — Google Business Profile and local visibility for businesses whose customers come from the neighbourhood.
  • Honest reporting — rankings, traffic and what it means. If a measure didn't work, you'll hear that too.
On-page SEOAmazon SEOSearch ConsoleLocal SEO
04

Websites & Shops — Build and Maintain

A site that loads fast, works on a phone and that you can still update in two years. Built to fit the job: hand-coded when it should be light, WordPress or Shopify when you need to edit it yourself.

  • Business websites — from a one-page presence to a multi-page site with blog, hand-coded or on WordPress depending on what you'll need to change yourself.
  • Online shops — WooCommerce and Shopify: product data, variants, payment providers, shipping rules, tax settings and a checkout that doesn't lose people.
  • Dropshipping setups — supplier connection, automated order flow and the ads that bring the traffic.
  • Relaunch & rescue — taking over an existing site that's slow, broken or built by someone who has since disappeared.
  • Maintenance & care — updates, backups, security, hosting, small changes. A monthly arrangement so you don't have to think about it.
  • Legal basics built in — cookie consent, privacy policy and imprint properly integrated (texts from your lawyer, integration from me).

05

Tracking, Data & Automation

If you can't measure it, you can't scale it. And if you measure it wrongly, you'll scale the wrong thing — which is worse.

  • Google Tag Manager & GA4 — a clean setup with events that mean something, not forty tags nobody dares to touch.
  • Conversion tracking — for Google, Meta and Microsoft, checked against the shop's own numbers so the platforms don't just report what you want to hear.
  • Consent & GDPR — consent banner, consent mode and tags that genuinely only fire when they're allowed to.
  • Dashboards & analysis — the numbers that matter in one view. For bigger data sets I use Python, SQL or R instead of a spreadsheet that takes a minute to open.
  • Automation — recurring manual work (reports, product data, price checks) turned into a script that just runs.
  • AI in the workflow — where AI genuinely saves time in content and analysis, and where it just produces text nobody wants to read. My thesis was on exactly this question.

Design, Print & Content

The visible half. A good product with bad pictures sells worse than a mediocre product with good ones — unfair, but that's how it works.

  • Print material — business cards, flyers, restaurant menus, brochures, posters and labels, delivered print-ready.
  • Product photography — clean product shots on white for the shop and Amazon, plus lifestyle images that show the product in use.
  • Image editing & infographics — retouching, cut-outs and the graphics that explain a product in the listing gallery.
  • 3D renderings — product visuals with KeyShot when the product doesn't exist yet or a photo can't show it.
  • Video editing — product and ad videos cut in DaVinci Resolve, in the formats each platform wants.
  • Brand basics — logo, colours, fonts and a small style guide so everything you publish looks like it belongs together.

Pricing

No package prices. On purpose.

I don't publish fixed packages, because a "website from €X" either overcharges the small project or shortchanges the big one. What a project costs depends on its size and complexity — and I can only judge that once I know what you're planning.

What drives the price

  • Scope: one landing page or a shop with 400 products
  • Complexity: standard setup or special requirements
  • Starting point: from scratch or fixing what exists
  • Timeline: normal schedule or urgent
  • Ongoing care: one-off project or monthly support

What you can count on

  • A written quote before anything starts
  • A fixed price for a fixed scope — no surprise invoices
  • Extra work is agreed beforehand, never afterwards
  • Free first call, whether or not it turns into a project
  • An honest no if I'm not the right fit for your project
